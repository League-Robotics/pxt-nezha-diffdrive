==============================================================
Does per-line delivery decay with REPLY LENGTH?  vevov, 2026-08-27
==============================================================
Question raised by radio-robot-lib-85: STATUS is now the longest reply
on the wire AND the verb you reach for when the link is in trouble. If
delivery tracks length, that is the wrong direction.

Design: four verbs of IDENTICAL arity (bare, unsequenced, zero fields),
so reply LENGTH is the only variable. Round-robin interleaved (not
blocked by form, so link drift cannot masquerade as a per-form effect).
n=50 each, 200 sends per arm. Wilson 95% CIs.

WHY THE INTERLEAVING MATTERS MORE THAN THE p-VALUE: the absolute rate
here is bad AND unstable, so all four arms must sample a common,
drifting link. Round-robin makes drift hit every arm equally, which is
what lets PING-vs-STATUS be a fair WITHIN-condition comparison despite
the link being in an unknown state. Run as four sequential blocks
instead and the same numbers would be uninterpretable -- "STATUS is
longer" would be inseparable from "the link got worse during block 4".

--- ARM 1: RADIO, torture relay pool -> channel 4 ---------------------
verb      reply B    delivered    rate    95% CI
PING           11     39/50      78.0%   [65%, 87%]
VER            10     34/50      68.0%   [54%, 79%]
ID             31     33/50      66.0%   [52%, 78%]
STATUS        109     27/50      54.0%   [40%, 67%]
ALL                  133/200     66.5%   [60%, 73%]

Two-proportion z-tests:
  PING(11B)  vs STATUS(109B)  z=2.53  p=0.011   <- SIGNIFICANT
  PING(11B)  vs ID(31B)       z=1.34  p=0.181
  ID(31B)    vs STATUS(109B)  z=1.22  p=0.221
  PING(11B)  vs VER(10B)      z=1.13  p=0.260   <- noise floor, as expected
                                                   (near-identical lengths)

--- ARM 2: CONTROL, wired USB via gauti ------------------------------
verb      reply B    delivered    rate
PING           12     49/50      98.0%
VER            10     50/50     100.0%
ID             31     50/50     100.0%
STATUS        109     50/50     100.0%    <- longest reply, PERFECT
ALL                  199/200     99.5%

==============================================================
CONCLUSIONS
==============================================================
1. The FIRMWARE is not length-limited. On a clean link every form
   answers every time, including the 109-byte STATUS at 50/50. Whatever
   is happening on the radio is not the handler dropping long replies.

2. A length effect on the RADIO is real ACROSS THE EXTREMES but not
   step-to-step. PING vs STATUS separates at p=0.011 over a ~10x length
   span; every adjacent pair sits in the noise (p=0.18-0.26). Honest
   statement: length matters at the STATUS end; this run does NOT
   resolve the ordering between VER, ID and each other.

3. The radio link is the dominant term in everything, by a wide margin:
   66.5% vs 99.5%. That is ~13x the ~5% loss protocol.md S8.0 names as
   its design assumption.

4. THERE WAS PROBABLY NEVER A REGRESSION. Two earlier drafts of this
   file got this wrong in two different ways; this is the third and it
   retracts the premise, not just the wording.

   Draft 1 asserted "ch4 degraded since this morning."
   Draft 2 retracted the SIZE of the drop (the morning measurement
     partly counted the 20 Hz beacon, which sprint 024 deleted -- the
     instrument was removed, so the two numbers are not commensurable).
   Draft 3 (this one) retracts the EVENT. Run the morning figures
     through their own confidence intervals:

       morning ID       8/8   point 100%   exact 95% CI [63.1%, 100%]
       morning VER      6/6   point 100%   exact 95% CI [54.1%, 100%]
       morning STATUS   5/6   point 83.3%
       tonight                66.5%, 75.0%, 83.3%  (three runs, n>=60)

   **8/8 cannot establish a rate above ~63%.** It is entirely consistent
   with a true rate of 65-70%. And the morning's STATUS figure, 5/6 =
   83.3%, is NUMERICALLY IDENTICAL to tonight's best run, 50/60 = 83.3%.
   Morning and tonight are not merely compatible -- one figure matches
   exactly.

   So the question was never "what broke ch4 today." It is **"why has
   ch4 apparently always been at ~75%, and why did we believe
   otherwise?"** Different question, different prior: nothing changed,
   the link has been mediocre all along, and small-n spot checks made it
   look fine. Nothing in today's sprint history is a suspect.
   (Reframe owed to radio-robot-lib-85.)

5. THE DRIFT IS THE PHENOMENON. Three runs, same firmware, same
   channel, same evening:

       66.5%  (length experiment,       n=200)
       75.0%  (relay discriminator,     n=240)
       83.3%  (beacon test, OFF arm,    n=60)

   ~17 points inside two hours. Two consequences:

   * ANY STATIC EXPLANATION IS DEAD, and this -- not the clustering
     below -- is what rules them out. Relay unit identity, antenna, and
     placement are all FIXED properties; a fixed property cannot
     produce a rate that moves 17 points while it does not change.
   * Any future comparison MUST be interleaved or simultaneous.
     Sequential A/B on this link is worthless. That is why the length
     experiment above round-robins, and it is why that design choice
     does more work than its p-value does.

   Characterising the drift -- a long interleaved run across hours, to
   find its period -- is now the highest-value measurement available. A
   period is the strongest available clue to what is duty-cycling.

6. CANDIDATES, AND THEIR STATUS.

   a. RELAY UNIT -- RULED OUT. guvov 89/120 (74.2%), zetog 91/120
      (75.8%), n=120 each. NOTE: the clustering is NOT what rules it
      out. Those runs were sequential on a link that drifts 17 points,
      so a real 5-point unit difference would have been invisible and
      the 1.6-point agreement is partly luck. What rules it out is (5):
      unit identity is static, the failure is time-varying. On the
      clustering argument, gozop and getez (never handed out in 8
      connects) would be live loose ends. On the drift argument they
      are not.

   b. BEACON DELETION -- RULED OUT, with no firmware change needed.
      `TLM POSE #1` makes the robot stream telemetry continuously: same
      radio, same continuous-TX mechanism, nothing to revert. Alternated
      by block so drift hits both arms.

          telemetry OFF (quiet)   50/60   83.3%  [72%, 91%]
          telemetry ON  (busy)    46/60   76.7%  [65%, 86%]
          956 telemetry frames seen during ON blocks (the arm worked)
          ON vs OFF: z=-0.91  p=0.361

      No effect, and the point estimate goes the WRONG way for the
      hypothesis. Conservative in the right direction, too: telemetry
      frames are LONGER than the 20 Hz ack lines were, so the ON arm put
      more airtime on the channel than the beacon ever did. If
      continuous TX helped, that arm should have shown it most.

      Deliberately NOT tested by flashing a beacon-restored hex: the
      stakeholder killed the beacon on purpose, and this rig bricked the
      robot twice earlier the same day. A diagnostic that needs no
      revert beats one that does.

   c. INTERFERENCE / something duty-cycled in the environment -- NOT
      YET TESTED, and now the leading candidate by elimination, since
      it is the only one on the list that can vary with time.

==============================================================
PRACTICAL
==============================================================
For interactive protocol work, prefer gauti's USB (99.5%) over the
torture relay (66.5%). gauti is wired to vevov ON the playfield, so it
is not a bench-only path -- it reaches the robot in situ.

A short STATUS form (ready/next/done/reason only, dropping connL/connR/
otos/wedge/flags/i2cf/cyc) would help IF the length effect holds up --
but see conclusion 4: fix or explain the link first. Shortening the
diagnostic to survive a link that was fine this morning is treating the
symptom.
